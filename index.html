AI data analysis you can follow, from the question to every number.

Upload your data and ask a question in plain words. BambooAI works through it in three stages: it helps you think the question through, does the analysis step by step in Python, and then checks its own work. Every stage stays visible, so you can follow how the answer was reached.

1Analysis of the analyst

Work out the question first

Every analysis starts from someone’s view of the problem. It shapes what gets measured and compared, and what counts as an answer. When a question goes straight to an AI model, the model supplies that view on its own, usually without saying so. A sound analysis of the wrong question still misleads.

The Architect is an optional step before the analysis. When it is switched on, nothing runs straight away. It talks the question through with you, much as a tutor would, and writes down what you agree on as a brief: the question the analyst will be asked, and what the result will be compared with.

The conversation
The Architect first finds out why you are asking: to make a decision, to test something you suspect, or to learn about a subject. It explains what you need to know before asking anything, offers choices where they help, and accepts “I don’t know” as an answer. The analysis starts when you are ready.
What your view rests on
The reasons you give are looked up in the loaded data and marked holds, does not hold or cannot be checked. A look-up can only count, list or total what is there; it never runs the analysis or tells you what the data will show.
Other explanations
Up to two other explanations can be tested alongside your own. The Architect may suggest one in the conversation, or propose one that a column in your data can tell apart, which is tested only if you tick it. When data is loaded, chance, measurement error and the way the cases were chosen are always tested too.
Your saved analyses
The analyses you save are the Architect’s memory. When a new question touches the same subject, it looks through them and shows what it finds as exact quotes, each with its date. In the picture above it found two earlier analyses of the same sequence. If you build on one, the analyst receives it as an earlier result to test rather than as a fact.
Figure drafts
If you ask what the result will look like, a draft of each figure appears in the brief before anything runs. When the shape follows from a rule, as it does here, the draft is computed. When only your data can give it, the draft is drawn on made-up numbers and labelled that way.
The question as the analyst reads it
The Architect words the question for the analyst as the conversation goes. Before the analysis starts, a separate model reads it with only the information the analyst will have, and anything it cannot make sense of goes back to the Architect to be reworded.

Everything in the brief can be changed by hand, and every change is kept as a step you can go back to. The Architect’s messages are written by a model, so the brief is yours to correct. Once the analysis starts, it is held to the brief: if the data cannot provide what the brief asks for, the analyst stops and asks you instead of substituting something else.

2Analysis of the data

See every step

Once the question is settled, the analyst starts work. Each step appears in the app as it happens: the code it wrote, what that code printed and any attempt that failed. You can open any step to see exactly what it did.

The numbers and the context around them

Numbers tell you what happened. The notes people write around them often tell you why. BambooAI reads both, and every passage it reads keeps its place in the source, so you can check a quote against the original.

Hard data

Measurements and records of any kind, from lab results and sensor logs to survey scores and genetic data. Upload CSV, Parquet, JSON or Excel (.xlsx) files, several at once, or load from a connected data source.

Soft data

Notes alongside the data
Comments, replies, daily notes and plans that people wrote alongside the data, each kept with its date and author. They load automatically from connected apps that keep them.
Your documents
Attach up to four papers, reports or interview transcripts to a thread. They’re split into numbered passages, and tables in them become data you can analyse.
The web
BambooAI can look up published figures during the analysis, with links to where they came from.

In one analysis

Your codebook
Each note is read once, translated into English if needed, and tagged with codes from a codebook you control. If nothing fits, BambooAI suggests a new code and you decide whether to keep it.
Next to the numbers
The notes and their codes sit in the Python session as tables, so the analysis can line them up with your data. For example, the notes from the days around a sudden change in the measurements.
Easy to check
A look-up brings passages back checked word for word against the source. The report cites them by place, like [C.417] for a note or [D1.17] for a document, next to the [cell 6] behind a number, and warns you when a citation doesn’t match anything. When it counts codes, it calls that the model’s reading of the notes.
Quick, Deep or Adaptive
Pick how much effort a question deserves: a quick lookup, a full analysis, or a longer investigation with a reviewer. Each mode has a budget of turns and cost, and the analyst knows it.
Follow-ups and branches
A follow-up carries on where the last run ended, with its variables still there. Branch off any earlier analysis and see them all on the thread map.
Ask about a figure
Every figure has a chat button. Your question starts a follow-up run, with the picture included when the model is set up to read images.

BambooAI was built for data where the same things are measured again and again, and where you need to know where every number came from. It works on any tabular data.

3Analysis of the analysis

Check the work

An AI model can write a long analysis in minutes, much faster than anyone can read it closely. So BambooAI keeps a full record of the work: the code, what it printed, the working notes and every model call. When the analysis ends, it checks the report against that record.

The report
It says what was found, how sure the analysis is and what it couldn’t establish. Each number points to the cell that printed it, and each quote to its place in the source. A plain-language version comes with it, and either can be saved as a PDF.
Number checks
Every number in the report is compared with what the code printed, and the plain-language version with the technical one. A number that doesn’t match is flagged, not silently corrected.
Replay
The code behind the cited results is run again in a fresh Python session, and the numbers it prints are compared with the original run.
The result and the brief
If the question was framed with the Architect, the brief shows how the result turned out: whether you saw what you expected, and what each other explanation came to. Results the brief doesn’t account for are reported in the answer under Did not fit.
For a question about how soon two runs of the Lorenz system move apart, the end of the brief might read:
How it turned out You expectedthe distance first passes 1 at t = 25.2 [cell 3]not met Also checkedPart of the gap comes from the integrator’s rounding, not from the system: at a tolerance 1,000 times tighter the distance also passes 1 at t = 25.2 [cell 4]

Trace any number back to the data

When an analysis finishes, BambooAI reads its code and the session’s record and draws a map from your data to each number. The map is built by fixed rules, with no AI involved, so the same analysis always gives the same map, and it never shows anything the code didn’t do.

Worth checking

The map also points out what deserves a second look: inputs that were offered but never used, values typed straight into the code that results depend on, and results that came out empty or were replaced.

For example, the map might say:

CUT = 80000.0 is typed into cell 2, not read from the data; all 12 results rest on it

The map shows how a number was made, not whether it’s right. That call is still yours, and the reviewer’s.

You can open it from the summary at the end of an analysis, from a [cell n] citation in the report, or from the eye icon on any cell in the notebook.

The reach report
It lists anything the code touched outside your data: web addresses and how much was sent or received, files, the environment variables it read and any programs it started. Each entry says whether your question asked for it.
For example, the summary might say:
cell 17 sent 42 KB to an address the question never named
The reviewer
In Adaptive mode, a second model with its own instructions reviews the evidence every eight turns. It can ask for another test, narrow a claim, or tell the analyst it is time to write the report.
Usage and logs
Every model call is logged with its prompt, tokens, time and cost, and you can open the exact prompt behind any step in the current thread. The Usage view shows where the money and the time went, by analysis, role and model.

↻After the analysis

Next questions, memory and routines

A finished analysis often leads to another question. BambooAI can suggest where to go next, keeps the analyses you save for the Architect to draw on, and can turn a method that worked into a routine you run on new data.

Next questions
When a run ends, Next questions offers five directions: refine focus, shift angle, test boundaries, blend elements and follow emergence. Each gives up to five questions based on what the analysis found or left open, and you can edit any of them before running it.
Memory
When you frame a question on a subject you have worked on before, the Architect can draw on the analyses you saved, and where they disagree it says so and asks which way to go. Deleting a saved analysis removes it from memory.
Routines
A finished analysis can be saved as a routine and run on new data without a model. Each run is checked and compared with the one before, and a step that can’t be computed on the new data affects only the results that depend on it.

The idea behind it

BambooAI is built on the view that AI-generated analysis should be open to the same scrutiny as any other analytical work. Reproducible calculations are an important part of that, but the question, the choice of methods, the assumptions and the interpretation need to stay open to examination too, and they still need a person to think them through.

Try it hosted, or run it yourself

It’s the same BambooAI either way. The difference is where it runs and which models it uses.

Hosted

Invitation beta

  • Nothing to install. It runs in your browser, on your own data.
  • Your code runs in a private sandbox, one per account.

Open source

MIT licence, on GitHub and PyPI

  • The whole app runs on your computer. The code runs in a Docker container there, and your files stay with you.
  • Pick the model for each role: OpenRouter, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Groq, Mistral, xAI or DeepSeek, or your own models through Ollama or vLLM.
  • Prompts go to whichever providers you choose. Run every role on your own models and they stay on your network too.
